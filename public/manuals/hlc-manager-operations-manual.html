Manager Operations Manual

Daily operating standard for HLC owners, managers, dispatchers, and authorized operators.

Print / Save as PDF

1. Daily opening check

  1. Open Dashboard and review alerts, KPIs, upcoming appointments, and unresolved work.
  2. Review Leads and identify requests waiting for scope/review.
  3. Review Workflow for blocked canonical stages.
  4. Review Automations for failed, paused, or approval-required items.
  5. Review Call Center/Messages for missed calls, communication outcomes, and follow-ups.
  6. Review Calendar for today and the next business day.

2. Golden workflow

Operate work in this order unless the product explicitly supports another path: Request → Lead → LeadScope/Estimate → Provider eligibility/offer → Provider acceptance → Appointment → Job → Completion → Review/referral/community continuation.

Do not create downstream records merely to make a dashboard look complete. Canonical state is the source of truth.

3. Lead and estimate management

Confirm contact details, service need, timing, property context, and available evidence. Build or review the scope before assigning work. Estimates should distinguish assumptions from confirmed conditions and should not promise final pricing when field verification is still required.

4. Provider operations

Use provider eligibility, service areas, availability, profile status, and explicit acceptance. Never fabricate credentials, rankings, assignment acceptance, availability, licensing, or completion. If a provider declines or cannot serve the request, return the workflow to the proper coordination stage rather than silently reassigning history.

5. Scheduling

Appointments must reflect a real commitment. Confirm time window, provider/technician, customer context, and job relationship. Use the canonical reschedule path so history and linked records remain accurate.

6. Communications and Google Voice

HLC is the operational record; Google Voice can be the live carrier surface. Keep HLC open beside Google Voice on Mac, or return to HLC after an iPhone call, to record outcome, notes, and follow-up. Respect consent, suppression, purpose, and channel requirements before automated outreach.

7. Automation policy

Automation should remove repetitive work without bypassing judgment or authorization. Deterministic, read-only, notification, and scheduled maintenance actions may run automatically when configured. Actions requiring customer commitment, provider acceptance, money, sensitive record changes, or management judgment must retain their required confirmation/approval boundary.

8. Media and documentation

Ask for useful evidence, not random uploads. Preferred sequence: wide context → close issue → labels/model numbers → surrounding damage/safety context → short narrated video only when motion/sound/location context adds value. Documents and media should be attached to the correct HLC record and workspace.

9. Customer and resident experience

Use clear status language. “Request received” is not “provider assigned.” “Estimate prepared” is not “customer approved.” “Checkout returned” is not “subscription active.” “Call started” is not “call completed.” HLC should only state outcomes supported by recorded evidence.

10. AI team

Kendrell supports command, risk, approvals, and owner-level summaries. Dion supports leads, jobs, providers, workflows, call-center operations, and analytics. Diamond supports onboarding, messages, community, reviews, referrals, and customer experience. Agents assist; HLC permissions and canonical state govern.

11. Team and access

Every worker uses an individual account. Owners/managers assign the minimum role needed. Remove access promptly when a person no longer requires it. Never share passwords or use a management account as a generic team login.

12. Incident management

For wrong-workspace data, suspected unauthorized access, payment anomalies, automation outside policy, or repeated write failures: pause the affected workflow, preserve timestamps/record IDs/screenshots, identify blast radius, and escalate before broad changes.

13. Daily closeout

  1. Resolve or assign all urgent follow-ups.
  2. Confirm completed jobs are actually complete and documented.
  3. Confirm tomorrow’s appointments.
  4. Review failed automation/communications.
  5. Review unresolved customer/provider messages.
  6. Record any technical issue requiring technician escalation.
